# Classic Margherita Pizza

## Overview

Two 30 cm pizzas with a slow-fermented dough, uncooked San Marzano tomato sauce,
fresh mozzarella and basil. The dough needs 24 hours in the refrigerator, so start
the day before. Active work is under 40 minutes.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Quantity | Unit | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type 00 flour | 500 | g | or strong bread flour |
| Water | 325 | ml | at 20 C, for a 65 percent hydration dough |
| Fine sea salt | 12 | g | added after the first mix |
| Fresh yeast | 3 | g | or 1 g instant dried yeast |
| San Marzano tomatoes | 400 | g | tinned whole, crushed by hand, not cooked |
| Fresh mozzarella | 250 | g | torn and drained 30 minutes |
| Fresh basil leaves | 12 | leaves | added after baking |
| Extra virgin olive oil | 2 | tbsp | for drizzling |
| Semolina flour | 2 | tbsp | for launching the pizza |

## Method

1. Mix the flour, water and yeast until no dry flour remains, then rest for 20
   minutes. Add the salt and knead for 8 minutes until smooth.
2. Bulk ferment at room temperature for 2 hours, then divide into two 410 g balls
   and refrigerate for 24 hours.
3. Take the dough out 2 hours before baking and let it come to room temperature.
4. Preheat the oven with a pizza stone or steel on the top shelf to 250 C, which is
   480 F, and leave it for a full 45 minutes after the oven reaches temperature.
   The stone must be saturated with heat, not merely hot.
5. Crush the tomatoes by hand with a pinch of salt. Do not blend them and do not
   cook them.
6. Stretch a dough ball to 30 cm on a semolina-dusted peel, spread 4 tablespoons of
   sauce leaving a 2 cm border, and scatter over half the mozzarella.
7. Bake for 6 to 8 minutes until the crust is blistered and the cheese has just
   melted.
8. Finish with basil leaves and a drizzle of olive oil after the pizza leaves the
   oven, never before.

## Notes & Substitutions

A domestic oven that only reaches 220 C will still work but needs 10 to 12 minutes
and produces a drier crust. Switching on the grill for the final minute helps.

Low-moisture mozzarella can be used and actually browns more predictably, but the
result is no longer a true margherita. This recipe contains gluten and dairy and
cannot be made gluten-free without changing the dough entirely.
